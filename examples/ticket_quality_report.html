Claude-generated insights

Most tickets are not ready for development

Only 3 of 50 tickets (6%) are rated ready. 30 need clarification and 17 are not ready. Average clarity is 3.08 out of 5, and no ticket scored 5.

Recommendation: Add a lightweight definition-of-ready check before tickets enter the sprint backlog, requiring a clear problem statement, scope, and testable acceptance criteria. Review the 47 tickets that are not ready and send the reporters targeted questions.

Supporting tickets: DEMO-124, DEMO-136, DEMO-104, DEMO-129

Missing or partial acceptance criteria are the dominant gap

Acceptance criteria are sufficient in only 3 tickets. 28 are partial and 19 are missing. In the samples, missing criteria come with no reproduction steps, so developers cannot verify a fix. Partial criteria leave key terms undefined, such as which payment states are reconciled, how 'critical' tests are identified, and what happens to non-critical failures.

Recommendation: Use a ticket template that requires measurable acceptance criteria. For bugs, include reproduction steps, expected versus actual behavior, and error details. Define terms such as 'critical' and 'reconciled' explicitly in the criteria.

Supporting tickets: DEMO-124, DEMO-136, DEMO-104, DEMO-129

Even ready tickets carry minor unresolved ambiguities

Clarity scores of 4 are the most common (28 tickets). Tickets rated ready still leave details open, such as sort direction, the scope of the fix across endpoints, the out-of-stock response format, and the retry mechanism.

Recommendation: Hold a short refinement pass on tickets that score 4 to settle edge-case details, such as response formats and scope boundaries, and record the answers in the ticket. This should move many of them to ready with little effort.

Supporting tickets: DEMO-109, DEMO-134, DEMO-104
